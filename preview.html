Why I Wrote This Book

I was sitting in Bible study.

While studying Genesis, particularly Genesis 1:3-5, a man in the group — a physicist — said to the pastor, “I know it wasn’t a literal day. I know the earth is millions of years old.” The pastor replied, “Nobody knows what the Creator meant by a day.”

I was surprised. Not by the physicist — by the pastor’s answer. That moment started a question that would not let me go: Was the Creator’s writing actually confusing… or had we simply been taught to treat it that way? If this one plain statement could be explained away so easily, what else had I accepted without checking? Was I allowing my family to be guided by tradition instead of by what the Scriptures actually say — in both the Old Testament and the New Testament?

I realized four things:

I had only been assuming what I had been taught was solid truth.

I had trusted family beliefs without question.

I had entrusted others with my family’s eternal safety.

I had never truly read the text in full context.

That realization led to thirty years of careful study. I read the Hebrew Scriptures from Genesis to Malachi and the New Testament from Matthew to Revelation. I used Strong’s Concordance to examine every word in the original Hebrew and Greek. I visited other congregations to see what they taught. And at the end of 2020, I noticed something I had never seen before.

There were clear differences between what the Creator said in the Old Testament and what the New Testament claimed about Him.

I reviewed the passages again and again. The differences remained. I confirmed them with my wife. She told me, “You’ve got the skill — put it in a book. For our family, and for anyone else who might listen.”

Then her cancer returned. I set every note aside and cared for her until she was gone. She is with the Creator now. And I am doing what she asked.

— Ezekiel 33:7–9 (WEB)

7 “So you, son of man, I have set you a watchman to the house of Israel. Therefore hear the word from my mouth, and give them warnings from me.

8 When I tell the wicked, ‘O wicked man, you will surely die,’ and you don’t speak to warn the wicked from his way, that wicked man will die in his iniquity, but I will require his blood at your hand.

9 Nevertheless, if you warn the wicked of his way to turn from it, and he doesn’t turn from his way; he will die in his iniquity, but you have delivered your soul.”

This book is not an argument. It is not an attack. It is a question — the same question that hit me thirty years ago in that Bible study: What if the people teaching us haven’t actually read in context what the Creator said — and are simply following tradition?

Everything in these pages has been in the Bible all along. I found it by learning to read each passage in its full context, letting the text speak for itself.

Whether you engage these questions is your decision. I wrote them because my wife believed you deserved the chance.

A Note on Language

The Hebrew Scriptures use multiple names and titles for the one being who made heaven and earth — including God, LORD, Yahweh, Adonai, and Elohim. The World English Bible renders several of these as Yahweh. Whether that is the correct vocalization of the name is a matter of long-standing dispute, and tradition holds that the name is not to be spoken aloud. The scripture quotations in this book are left exactly as the WEB translates them.

When this book refers to the Hebrew Scriptures’ speaker outside the quoted verses, it uses the word Creator. Every time you see Creator in the commentary of these pages, it points to the same being the Scriptures identify: the one who made everything, who was there before anything else existed.

When the New Testament makes a claim, you can ask: Does it match what the Creator said?

You are encouraged to verify every passage in your own Bible.

How to Read This Book

This book follows a simple pattern in every chapter. Each chapter begins with a claim or teaching from the New Testament.

Then the Old Testament is opened — in full context — so you can read what the Creator said on the same subject. Minimal commentary is provided only to identify speaker, subject, and context. This book reads every passage in its plain, contextual meaning. The standard for this method is drawn from the Scriptures themselves — specifically the passages presented in the Prelude.

The questions in this book focus your attention on what the text identifies — the speaker, the subject, the authority, and the context. They are observations, not arguments. They ask what is on the page, not what the reader should believe about it. Where the text leaves room for more than one reading, that is also a finding worth marking. The book’s only standard is the one already named: Assumption Is Not Confirmation. The reader’s answer is the reader’s answer.

A Letter to Family and Friends

If someone you love gave you this book, they did not give it to hurt you.

They gave it because they read it — and something in these pages mattered enough to share with you.

This book does not tell you what to believe. It does not ask you to leave anything. It asks you to read — to see what the Creator said in His own words, in the passages where He said them — and to decide for yourself.

The person who gave you this book found it worth sharing. Now they are offering you the same opportunity. Not to agree with them. Not to follow them. To read for yourself and arrive at your own answer.

That is all this book asks. That is all they are asking.

Whatever you decide — they love you. That has not changed.
